5. Consolidation: why settlement takes time ​

Load first, drain afterward ​

Place a load on a saturated column confined by rigid side walls. Initially, little water has time to leave, and pore pressure rises. Water then drains through an open boundary. As pressure falls, the skeleton carries more effective compression and settlement grows. This delayed deformation is consolidation.

What the benchmarks teach ​

The Terzaghi benchmark compares this series to the finite element calculation (Terzaghi, 1943). Mandel's problem shows that pressure can temporarily rise at some locations even after a fixed load is applied: mechanical redistribution changes the local storage response. Do not generalize the monotonic Terzaghi profiles to every geometry. Cryer's sphere and De Leeuw's cylinder extend the checks to radial geometries.

These cases verify particular equations and discretizations. Agreement with them does not establish that a chosen material law describes every experimental specimen. Dangla chapter 6 develops the initial–boundary value viewpoint (Dangla, undated).

Check your understanding. Doubling specimen height while keeping single drainage and the same material multiplies the characteristic consolidation time by four.
